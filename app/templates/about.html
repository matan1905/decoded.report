01

What This Is

decoded.report is a free screening tool for US-listed companies. You type a ticker and it reads that company's own SEC filings (annual reports, quarterlies, 8-Ks, registration statements, insider forms) and produces a cited report: a risk index from 0 to 100, every red-flag phrase it found this year, a share-count history that shows dilution in the open, how many months of cash the company has left at its current burn, the insider paper trail, and an outside-records pass over domains, sanctions lists and institutional holders.

The point is not to tell you what to buy. It is to compress hours of EDGAR reading into something you can verify in minutes. Every number links back to the document it came from, so you never have to take our word for it.

02

Who Runs It

decoded.report is an independent project. It is not a broker, a fund, an investment adviser, or affiliated with any issuer it covers. There is no pay-to-play: companies cannot pay to change a score, remove a flag, or be featured. The reports are generated from the same public filings that anyone can pull from the SEC's EDGAR system.

03

Editorial Standards

  • Cite or drop it. A claim that cannot be tied to a specific filing, form and date does not go on the page.
  • No forecasts. We describe what a company reported and what it has done. We do not predict prices or returns.
  • Show the negative and the neutral. A clean screen is reported as clean. A dormant flag stays visible even when it is old.
  • Deterministic by design. Scores come from fixed, published rules applied to the same inputs for everyone. There is no editorial hand on the number.
  • Corrections. If a report is wrong because of a parsing error or a stale cache, tell us and it gets fixed. Email contact@decoded.report.
04

How We Make Money

The reports, the guides and the tools are free. The site is supported by display advertising served by Google AdSense. Ads are labeled, kept separate from the report content, and never influence a score. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not require an account to use the site.

05

Data Sources

Primary source is the SEC's EDGAR system: company submissions, financial statements, 8-K and other current reports, registration statements and insider Forms 3/4/5. Supporting records come from public registries and databases, including the US Treasury OFAC sanctions list, RDAP and archived WHOIS records, certificate transparency logs, the GLEIF legal-entity registry, and public market-data providers for prices, splits, float and short interest. Full detail lives on the methodology page.
