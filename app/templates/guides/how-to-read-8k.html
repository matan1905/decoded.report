A Form 8-K is the company telling the market that something happened between its regular quarterly reports. It is not a quarterly update and it is not a press release. It is a legal filing with a deadline, and the deadline is the reason it is worth reading: for most reportable events a US company has to file within four business days.

The four-business-day rule

The SEC set the deadline at four business days for most items when it overhauled Form 8-K in 2004. Day one is the first business day after the event. There is no twelve-day extension under Rule 12b-25 for the covered items, which is exactly the point: the deadline is meant to force fast disclosure. The exceptions are the items that report earnings (2.02), Regulation FD disclosures (7.01), and voluntary disclosures (8.01), which have no four-day clock, and a handful of items with special timing.

Practically, this means a fresh 8-K is often the fastest signal that something material changed, usually faster than the company's own public relations.

The items that change the story

An 8-K is organized by item number, and the number tells you what kind of event it is. Most items are routine mechanics. A few are the ones to stop on.

Serious

  • Item 1.03, Bankruptcy or Receivership. The company has filed for bankruptcy or is in receivership. This is about as direct as disclosure gets.
  • Item 3.01, Notice of Delisting or Failure to Satisfy a Continued Listing Rule. The exchange has told the company it no longer meets a listing standard, often a minimum share price or minimum equity test. A delisting notice is a countdown, not an eviction, but it forces action.
  • Item 4.02, Non-Reliance on Previously Issued Financial Statements. The company is telling you that numbers it already published should no longer be trusted because of an error. This is one of the loudest signals a filing can send.
  • Item 4.01, Changes in Certifying Accountant. The auditor resigned, was dismissed, or declined to stand for reappointment. Sometimes routine. Sometimes the canary.
  • Item 3.02, Unregistered Sales of Equity Securities. Shares were sold without a registered offering, often in a private placement or on conversion of debt. Frequently the first sighting of dilution.
  • Item 2.04, Triggering Events That Accelerate a Financial Obligation. A lender or counterparty can now demand faster repayment. A liquidity shock.

Worth reading

  • Item 2.01, Completion of an Acquisition or Disposition of Assets. The company bought or sold something significant.
  • Item 2.03, Creation of a Direct Financial Obligation. New debt or a new lease, material enough to disclose.
  • Item 2.06, Material Impairments. A write-down of assets.
  • Item 5.01, Changes in Control. Control of the company has changed hands, with all the change-of-direction that implies.
  • Item 5.02, Departure or Appointment of Directors and Officers. A CEO, CFO or other named executive left or arrived. Sudden senior departures are worth a look.

Mostly routine

  • Item 2.02, Results of Operations and Financial Condition. The quarterly or annual earnings release. Scheduled and expected, though the numbers matter.
  • Item 7.01, Regulation FD Disclosure. Information the company is deliberately sharing publicly to satisfy fair disclosure rules.
  • Item 8.01, Other Events. Voluntary, no deadline, and not always material. Read it, do not infer from the label.

How to read one in five minutes

  1. Open the filing and read the whole item list first. Two or three lines tell you what kind of filing this is.
  2. For any item above, click into the exhibit. The 8-K text is a summary; the agreement itself is the detail.
  3. Check the filing date against the event date. A late filing is its own small signal.
  4. Ask what it changes about the company's cash, its share count, or its listing. Those are the three things that move an equity story.

The report scans the recent 8-Ks for a company, collapses them into plain language and flags the items that actually change the situation, so a routine earnings 8-K does not drown out an Item 4.02 sitting right next to it.
